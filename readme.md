## ⚡ Powering the AI Era: An Analysis of U.S. Electric Utilities

The exponential growth of artificial intelligence has triggered a parallel surge in institutional demand for electricity. Over the past three years, this demand has reached levels significant enough to reshape long-term energy strategy in the United States.

Recent developments point to a potential **nuclear and grid-capacity renaissance**:

* The restart of the **Three Mile Island** nuclear plant in Pennsylvania to supply Microsoft’s data centers
* A deal between **NextEra Energy** and Google to revive the **Duane Arnold** nuclear facility in Iowa
* An **$80 billion** nuclear investment agreement between the U.S. government and **Westinghouse Electric Company**

These examples highlight a clear trend: whether through nuclear power or broader grid expansion, the U.S. energy system must scale rapidly to meet the demands of AI and their power-hungry corollary: data centers.

---

## 📊 Project Objective

This project analyzes **electric utility companies in the S&P 500** to better understand:

* The **scale of their electricity production capacity**
* The **composition of their energy mix** (nuclear, gas, solar, wind, etc.)
* Their **historical performance and growth trends**
* Which companies may be structurally positioned to benefit from rising electricity demand

---

## 🧩 Data Sources & Methodology

The core of the analysis combines two key data sources:

* **EIA Form 860 (2024)** — detailed plant-level data for all U.S. power plants, including:

  * Geographic location (latitude & longitude)
  * Nameplate and summer capacity (MW)
  * Fuel type (nuclear, gas, solar, etc.)
* **10-K filings** of S&P 500 utility companies

A central challenge lies in the structure of the EIA data:
power plants are listed under **subsidiary entities**, not their publicly traded parent companies.
For example, all Ameren (AEE) plants appear under its subsidiary *Union Electric Company*.

### Key Steps:

1. **Map subsidiaries to parent companies**
2. Aggregate plant-level capacity to the parent-company level
3. Analyze each company’s total capacity and energy mix
4. Compare structural positioning with historical financial performance

---

## 📈 Portfolio Construction

To complement the fundamental analysis, the project constructs a **Markowitz efficient portfolio** using selected utility stocks.
While this exercise is largely academic, given the lack of cross-sector diversification, it provides insight into:

* Risk concentration within the utility sector
* Volatility reduction through intra-sector allocation
* Trade-offs between capacity scale, energy mix, and financial performance

---

## 🔍 Why This Matters

As AI-driven electricity demand continues to rise, utility companies with scalable infrastructure, favorable energy mixes, and regulatory advantages may play a critical role in the next phase of U.S. energy expansion. This project aims to provide a **data-driven framework** for evaluating those companies and understanding how the grid is evolving under AI-era pressures.
